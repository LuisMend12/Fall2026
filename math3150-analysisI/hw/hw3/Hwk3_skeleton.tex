\section*{Problem 1 (10 points)}
Prove that for any $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$,
\[
  \bigl|\,|x|-|y|\,\bigr| \le |x-y| .
\]

\begin{proof}
We start from the identity
\[
  x = (x-y) + y .
\]
Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality,
\[
  |x| = |(x-y)+y| \le |x-y| + |y| .
\]
Subtracting $|y|$ from both sides gives
\[
  |x| - |y| \le |x-y| .
\]
We can do the same thing starting from the identity
\[
  y = (y-x) + x .
\]
Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality,
\[
  |y| = |(y-x)+x| \le |y-x| + |x| .
\]
Subtracting $|x|$ from both sides gives
\[
  |y| - |x| \le |y-x| .
\]
Since $y-x = -(x-y)$ and $|-t| = |t|$ for every real $t$, we have
$|y-x| = |x-y|$.

Now let $a = |x|-|y|$ and $c = |x-y|$. Note that $c \ge 0$.
The first inequality says $a \le c$. In the second, the left-hand side is
$|y|-|x| = -a$ and the right-hand side is $|y-x| = c$, so it says
$-a \le c$, i.e.\ $a \ge -c$. Together,
\[
  -c \le a \le c .
\]
Since $c \ge 0$, this is equivalent to $|a| \le c$, that is,
\[
  \bigl|\,|x|-|y|\,\bigr| \le |x-y| ,
\]
which is the original statement.

\end{proof}
